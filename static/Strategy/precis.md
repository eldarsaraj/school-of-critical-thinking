# The School of Critical Thinking — Brand Precis

A working document defining the mission, positioning, audiences, and offerings of The School of Critical Thinking. This is the source-of-truth reference for website copy, marketing, and product decisions across all segments.

---

## 1. Mission

The School of Critical Thinking exists to teach people — at every age and in every context — how to think well in a world that increasingly does the thinking for them.

We treat critical thinking as a real discipline, not a soft skill. We teach it from first principles, with intellectual seriousness, and we build the curriculum end-to-end: from age eight to the executive boardroom, on the same foundations.

## 2. Core Idea

Our work is organized around one governing metaphor: **critical thinking as resistance to entropy**.

The world tends toward disorder. Information arrives unstructured. Patterns hide. Categories blur. Confident-sounding nonsense spreads faster than careful argument. AI systems generate plausible text at near-zero cost, accelerating the entropy.

Thinking critically is the act of pushing back. It is the work of *ordering* — making sense of what is chaotic, decomposing what is complex, modeling what is opaque, naming what is unnamed. To think critically is to resist the encroaching entropy of the world.

This metaphor is not decorative. It is the conceptual engine of the entire curriculum, present in *Seeing Patterns* (the kids' book), in *Resisting Entropy* (the adult book), and in every corporate program. It gives the brand a single coherent identity across audiences.

## 3. Pedagogical Approach

The curriculum is built on four foundations, drawn from a *computational* perspective on thinking:

- **Decomposition** — breaking down complex problems into manageable parts
- **Abstraction** — modeling the world through concepts, theories, and useful simplifications
- **Pattern recognition** — seeing the order beneath surface complexity
- **Algorithmic thinking** — building reusable thought processes that hold up under pressure

These are taught from first principles. We do not assume readers know what "critical thinking" means; we build the intuition from scratch. The same four foundations operate at every age — the examples, register, and stakes change, but the underlying skills do not.

**What makes this distinctive.** Most critical thinking education is anchored in philosophy and logic — taught by humanities professors, focused on formal reasoning or discourse analysis. Our approach is rooted in the same Platonic tradition of truth-seeking, but reframes it through a computational lens that is genuinely useful in the age of AI. We do not teach syllogisms. We teach how to think clearly about messy, uncertain, real problems.

## 4. The Three Audiences

The School of Critical Thinking serves three distinct audiences under one brand. Each gets its own offer, its own register, and its own page on the site — but all rest on the same intellectual foundation.

### 4.1 Families and Homeschoolers

Parents who want to give their children critical thinking skills they will not get anywhere else. The school system rarely teaches this directly; we do.

**Core offering:** A three-level book series spanning ages 8–16, with three books per level. *Seeing Patterns* (Book 1, ages 8–11) is in production. The series teaches pattern recognition, uncertainty, modeling, inference, and reasoning under conditions that match a child's developing cognition.

**Format:** Physical books designed for active engagement — readers are expected to write in them, mark them up, do the exercises. Each lesson includes a story, a thinking question, a thinking challenge, a real-world example, a thinking habit, and a small activity.

**Companion products:** Browser-based interactive courses (e.g., Python Detective, ages ~11) hosted on the site, using in-browser code execution to teach computational thinking through play.

### 4.2 Learners

Students and self-directed adults who are learning critical thinking on their own initiative, for stakes they personally care about. This audience self-selects: they are already motivated, they buy their own access, and they do not require institutional sales cycles.

Three sub-groups sit comfortably inside this audience:

**Test-prep students.** Standardized admissions tests reward exactly the skills the curriculum teaches — pattern recognition, logical reasoning, decomposition, and reasoning under uncertainty. The initial offering is a **dashboard for the SHSAT** (the entrance exam for New York City's specialized high schools), with potential expansion to other standardized tests over time (SAT, GRE, LSAT). The dashboard frames test preparation not as memorization or trick-learning, but as the application of real critical thinking skills to a high-stakes problem. This positioning is genuinely differentiated — most test prep is rote; ours is pedagogical.

**Older teens and pre-college students.** Learners who have aged past the children's book series but are not yet the audience for the adult book. Browser-based interactive courses (Python Detective and future successors) live here. Future expansion may include topical micro-courses, problem sets, and self-paced thinking workouts.

**Adult self-learners.** Readers of *Resisting Entropy* who want to keep going. Newsletter subscribers. People who buy thinking books on their own and want a community, a deeper curriculum, or structured practice. Future expansion may include cohort-based courses, written exercises, and (eventually) a platform offering.

**Core offering (current):** The SHSAT dashboard, the browser-based interactive courses, and the books themselves as self-study materials.

**Future expansion:** Cohort-based courses, certifications, platform-delivered curricula, and additional test-prep dashboards. This is the audience segment with the longest growth runway and the clearest path to a scalable platform offering.

### 4.3 Organizations

Companies, leadership teams, and L&D departments that recognize critical thinking — and especially *critical thinking with AI* — as the most undervalued skill in their workforce.

**Core offering:** Training, workshops, and keynotes that treat critical thinking as a serious professional competency. Built on the same intellectual foundation as the books, but pitched for working professionals making real decisions under real stakes.

**Anchor offerings (initial):**

- **Lunch & learn talks** (60–90 minutes). Single-topic, high-impact sessions designed to seed interest and demonstrate value. Example: *Why Reasonable Decisions Go Wrong: A Field Guide to Modern Paradoxes.*
- **Half-day and full-day workshops.** Hands-on application of critical thinking frameworks to real organizational problems. Pre-mortems, decomposition exercises, working through paradoxes and decision traps with the team's own cases.
- **Multi-session programs.** Structured cohort-based programs for leadership teams, decision-making groups, or teams integrating AI into their workflows.

**Signature themes** (the topics that distinguish us from generic L&D providers):

- *Thinking with AI without letting it think for you* — judgment, evaluation, and epistemic discipline in the age of LLMs
- *The hidden architecture of bad decisions* — paradoxes, traps, and the patterns behind organizational failure
- *Pattern recognition for leaders* — seeing what others miss in data, decisions, and dynamics
- *Decomposition and clarity* — breaking down complex problems before solving them
- *Reasoning under uncertainty* — probabilistic thinking for non-statisticians

The *Resisting Entropy* book serves as the intellectual foundation and credentialing artifact for this segment.

## 5. The Books

The two flagship books are not separate products. They are the two ends of a single curriculum, written for different audiences but resting on the same first principles.

**Seeing Patterns** (kids, ages 8–11). The first book in the children's series. Teaches pattern recognition as the foundational skill of critical thinking — across nature, language, behavior, data, and reasoning itself. Concludes with "The Pattern Mind."

**Resisting Entropy: Critical Thinking in the Age of AI** (adults). The flagship adult book. Teaches critical thinking from first principles through a computational lens — decomposition, abstraction, pattern recognition, algorithmic thinking — with chapters on truth, analogy, conditional thinking, uncertainty, data, Bayesian reasoning, and the relationship between order and chaos. The book's thesis: critical thinking is the existential practice of ordering a chaotic world, and is more urgent now than ever.

Together, the books make a claim no competitor can make: *we have built the curriculum from the ground up, for every stage of life, on a single coherent foundation.*

## 6. What We Are Not

Defining the negative space is as important as defining the positive.

- We are **not** a generic soft-skills training provider. We do not teach "communication skills" or "emotional intelligence."
- We are **not** a logic or formal reasoning school. We do not teach syllogisms, Venn diagrams, or fallacy lists as ends in themselves.
- We are **not** a productivity or self-help brand. We do not sell shortcuts, hacks, or motivational content.
- We are **not** a prompt-engineering shop. AI literacy is part of what we teach, but it is downstream of the deeper skill: thinking well.
- We are **not** an academic institution. The curriculum is rigorous, but the writing is plain, the tone is direct, and there are no citations cluttering the page.

## 7. Voice and Register

Across all audiences, the voice of The School of Critical Thinking is:

- **Intellectually serious without being academic.** No jargon. No citations. No hedging. We make real claims and defend them with examples.
- **Plain-spoken.** Short sentences when they will do. Long ones when they earn their length. Direct address to the reader.
- **Visually rich.** Critical thinking is partly visual thinking. Books, slides, and web pages should include charts, diagrams, illustrations, and visual scaffolding. Not as decoration — as a teaching tool.
- **Confident but not preachy.** We assume the reader is intelligent and underserved, not stupid and in need of saving.
- **Calibrated to audience.** Children's books are warm, playful, and full of stories. Adult books are crisper, drier, more direct. Corporate materials are sharper still — every sentence earns its place.

The register shifts across audiences; the voice does not.

## 8. Visual Identity Notes

The visual system reflects the entropy/order metaphor: structure emerging from chaos, signal pulled from noise.

- **Children's books:** Mixed-media collage by the author. Loose and chaotic in early chapters; clean and structured in later chapters. The illustrations themselves enact the cognitive arc of the curriculum.
- **Adult book:** Charts, graphs, and visual frameworks on every page. No purely decorative imagery. Every visual is a teaching tool.
- **Corporate materials:** Restrained, austere, professional. Strong typography. Diagrams over photographs. Conveys seriousness without coldness.

Recurring visual motifs across the brand: the magnifying glass, the key, the red thread, the question mark, the puzzle piece, the cracked mirror, the compass.

## 9. Strategic Positioning

The School of Critical Thinking is positioned to occupy a gap in three converging markets:

**The homeschool and supplemental education market**, where parents are increasingly aware that traditional schooling under-teaches reasoning skills, but most available "critical thinking" curricula are shallow, recycled, or workbook-driven.

**The test preparation and self-directed learning market**, where standardized tests reward critical thinking but are taught almost universally through rote drill and trick-learning. There is a clear opening for test prep that genuinely teaches — that treats the test as an application of real skills rather than a puzzle to be gamed. The SHSAT dashboard is the entry point; further test-prep products and self-paced courses extend the line.

**The corporate training market** (~$400B globally), where soft skills and AI literacy are the fastest-growing segments, but where existing offerings are dominated either by enormous platforms selling generic content or legacy consultancies selling prestige rather than depth. There is a clear gap for a serious independent practitioner with intellectual credibility and a real curriculum.

**The emerging "AI literacy" category**, where most training is reduced to prompt-engineering tips. The deeper need — *teaching humans to maintain judgment when working alongside systems that produce confident-sounding output at zero cost* — is largely unmet.

The unifying claim: *the same first principles that teach a nine-year-old to spot patterns teach a fourteen-year-old to crack the SHSAT and a forty-nine-year-old to spot what AI is hiding.* We are the only brand making this claim end-to-end.

## 10. Founder

The School of Critical Thinking is founded by Eldar Sarajlic, who writes the curriculum, writes the books, designs the materials, and delivers the corporate training. The brand is, at present, identified with him personally — and intentionally so. The intellectual identity of the work is more credible coming from a single voice than from an anonymous institution.

Long-term, the brand may grow into a small firm with additional facilitators, a platform, or licensed programs. The founder remains the author and intellectual lead.

## Appendix A: Website Information Architecture

The site (schoolofcriticalthinking.org) should make all three audiences visible from the homepage without forcing any of them to wade through irrelevant content.

**Homepage.** A unified statement of mission and the entropy/order thesis. Three clear pathways below: *For Families*, *For Learners*, *For Organizations*.

**For Families.** The book series, the interactive courses, descriptions of what children learn. Warm tone, sample lessons, age guidance, ordering links.

**For Learners.** The test-prep dashboards (SHSAT first), the interactive courses, the adult book, and any future self-paced offerings. Tone is direct and practical — these are people who came here to learn something specific and want to get to work.

**For Organizations.** A distinct, sober landing page. Lists the talk and workshop topics, the signature themes, the speaker bio framed for L&D buyers, and a short "what we believe about critical thinking at work" statement. Includes booking inquiry form.

**About.** One page. Tells the unified story: the founder, the curriculum, the through-line from age 8 to the boardroom. The single most important page on the site for establishing brand coherence.

**Books.** Both books presented together, with the connection between them made explicit. *Seeing Patterns* and *Resisting Entropy* are the two faces of one curriculum.

**Writing / Blog.** Optional but recommended. Long-form essays from the founder on critical thinking, AI, education, and decision-making. The mechanism by which the corporate audience discovers and trusts the brand.

## Appendix B: Quick Reference for Future Decisions

When evaluating any new product, page, partnership, or offering, the question is:

> Does this teach people to resist entropy — to bring order to chaos through better thinking?

If yes, it belongs.
If no, it does not — no matter how lucrative or adjacent.
