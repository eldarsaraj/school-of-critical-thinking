# PART I: WHAT THE SHSAT IS

The Specialized High Schools Admissions Test (SHSAT) is the sole admissions criterion for eight NYC specialized high schools.

Admissions are governed by the Hecht-Calandra Act.

Grades, attendance, recommendations, essays, extracurricular activities, and teacher evaluations are not used for admission decisions.

Schools include:

- Stuyvesant
- Bronx Science
- Brooklyn Tech
- Staten Island Tech
- HSMSE
- HSAS
- Queens Science at York
- Brooklyn Latin

LaGuardia High School is not admitted through the SHSAT.

---

# PART II: EXAM STRUCTURE

Current structure:

ELA:
- 57 questions

Math:
- 57 questions

Total:
- 114 questions

Testing time:
- 3 hours

Students allocate time between sections themselves.

Beginning with Fall 2026 administration, the SHSAT transitions to a computer-adaptive format while maintaining the same tested content.

---

# PART III: OFFICIAL DOE SCORING PROCESS

The DOE identifies three score types.

## Raw Score

Raw score = number of correct answers.

Important:

- Every scored question counts equally.
- No penalty for guessing.
- No penalty for incorrect answers.
- Leaving blanks is mathematically worse than guessing.

Raw scores are calculated separately for:

- ELA
- Math

---

## Scaled Score

DOE converts raw scores into scaled scores.

Reason:

Different test forms are not perfectly identical.

DOE uses:

### Calibration

Adjusts for difficulty differences between forms.

### Normalization

Fits scores into a statistical distribution.

Result:

Raw scores and scaled scores are NOT proportional.

One additional correct answer can produce:

- 3–4 scaled points in one range
- 10–20 scaled points in another range

The relationship is nonlinear.

---

## Composite Score

Composite Score:

ELA Scaled Score + Math Scaled Score

Admissions decisions are based on:

- Composite score
- School preference ranking
- Seat availability

Not raw scores.

---

# PART IV: WHAT THE DOE DOES NOT PUBLISH

The DOE does NOT publish:

- Exact scaling formulas
- Exact normalization equations
- Exact yearly conversion tables
- Exact adaptive-testing algorithms

As a result:

Nobody outside the DOE can accurately predict scaled scores.

All internet score calculators are estimates.

---

# PART V: FIELD TEST QUESTIONS

The SHSAT includes experimental questions.

Students cannot identify them during testing.

Community reports and tutoring analyses frequently reference approximately 10 experimental questions per section, though official operational details may vary over time.

Key implication:

Students should treat every question as scored.

---

# PART VI: ADMISSIONS ALGORITHM

Admissions occur after scoring.

Process:

1. Students rank schools.
2. Students receive composite scores.
3. Students are sorted from highest to lowest score.
4. Highest scorer receives first available preferred school.
5. Process repeats downward.
6. Final admitted student determines cutoff.

Therefore:

Cutoffs are NOT predetermined.

Cutoffs emerge from:

- Student performance
- Student preferences
- Available seats

---

# PART VII: NO PASSING SCORE

One of the biggest misconceptions.

There is:

- No passing score
- No failing score

Instead:

Each school develops a cutoff.

A score sufficient one year may not be sufficient the next.

# PART X: STRATEGIC IMPLICATIONS OF NONLINEAR SCALING

The DOE explicitly states:

Scaling becomes steeper near score extremes.

Meaning:

Going from:

55 → 56

may be worth substantially more scaled points than:

35 → 36

This has major implications.

Students near top performance levels benefit enormously from eliminating careless mistakes.

# PART XI: COMMON MYTHS

## Myth 1

"There is a conversion chart."

Reality:

No universal conversion chart exists.

---

## Myth 2

"90/114 always equals the same scaled score."

Reality:

Scaling changes every year.

---

## Myth 3

"Hard questions are worth more."

Reality:

Every scored question counts as one raw point.

---

## Myth 4

"Blank answers are safer."

Reality:

No guessing penalty exists.

---

## Myth 5

"Cutoffs are announced beforehand."

Reality:

Cutoffs are determined after admissions processing.

---

# PART XII: DISCOVERY PROGRAM

Discovery provides an alternate pathway.

Eligibility generally requires:

- SHSAT participation
- Economic disadvantage
- Attendance at eligible middle schools
- Score just below cutoff range

Students complete a summer program before receiving placement.

Discovery eligibility rules change periodically and should always be checked against current DOE guidance.

---

# PART XIII: PRACTICAL PREPARATION INSIGHTS

Strong consensus across tutors and experienced families:

Track:

- Raw scores
- Section performance
- Error patterns

Avoid obsessing over:

- Online score calculators
- Exact scaled-score predictions

Useful benchmark:

70% in both sections is often where students begin becoming competitive for lower-cutoff specialized schools.

80%+ generally puts students into the range where many schools become realistic possibilities.

Mid-to-high 80% performance is typically associated with the strongest schools.

---

# PART XIV: WHAT PARENTS MOST OFTEN MISUNDERSTAND

1. The SHSAT is rank-based, not pass/fail.

2. Admissions are relative to other students.

3. One-point improvements can matter dramatically near cutoffs.

4. School preference order matters.

5. Cutoffs emerge from seat allocation, not from a target score.

6. Raw-score percentages are often more useful than internet score estimates.

7. Stuyvesant has historically required both exceptional accuracy and consistency.

---

# FINAL CONCLUSIONS

The most important facts about the SHSAT are:

- Admissions are determined solely by SHSAT performance.
- Raw scores become scaled scores through a nonlinear DOE process.
- The exact conversion formula is not public.
- Composite scores determine admissions.
- School cutoffs vary every year.
- There is no passing score.
- Students should focus on maximizing correct answers, not predicting scaled scores.
- Historical cutoffs are useful for planning but never guarantee admission.
