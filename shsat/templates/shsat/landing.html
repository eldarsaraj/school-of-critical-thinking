See it in action

Here's how an SHSAT trap actually works.

A reading passage describes a scientist who initially doubted a new theory but, after reviewing the evidence, changed her position. The question asks: which best describes the scientist's approach?

Choice A: She was open-minded and revised her view based on evidence.
Choice B: She was skeptical and reluctant to accept new ideas.

Most students pick B. It's not wrong about the passage — she was skeptical. But B describes her initial reaction, not her overall approach. The question asks about the approach, which includes the revision. A is the answer.

This is a partly-true-but-wrong trap — the most common distractor type on the SHSAT. It gives you something genuinely from the passage, which is exactly what makes a student under time pressure grab it. Our error analysis catches this. It names the trap, shows how often your child falls for it, and tracks whether it resolves across tests.
