# SHSAT Prep — Competitor Benchmark & Disruption Map

*Prepared for schoolofcriticalthinking.org · Data gathered June 2026 from public pricing pages. Prices change often — re-verify before quoting publicly. SHSAT® is a registered trademark of the NYC DOE.*

---

## 1. The big picture

The SHSAT prep market splits into four tiers. Your opportunity is the gap between Tier 1 (cheap but generic) and the very expensive Tier 3/4 (effective but $1,500–$5,000).

| Tier | Type | Typical price | Who's here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Free / near-free self-study | $0–$25/yr | NYC DOE Handbook, Khan Academy, PASSNYC, TestPrepSHSAT free tier |
| 2 | **Digital self-serve platforms** ← *your arena* | $5–$300/mo | HighScores.ai, ArgoPrep, Study.com, TestingMom, Caddell on-demand |
| 3 | National hybrid course + tutoring | $275–$3,000 | Kaplan, Princeton Review |
| 4 | Premium local NYC tutoring | $1,080–$4,750 | Kweller, Khan's Tutorial, SamSchool, Smith Street |

The single most important market shift: **the SHSAT is now fully digital and adaptive** (drag-and-drop math, scrambled-paragraph ELA, real-time difficulty). Every paper-based prep resource is now partially obsolete. Whoever best simulates the *actual digital test interface* wins — and most of the field hasn't caught up.

## 3. What each does well (steal these)

**HighScores.ai — your #1 benchmark.** This is the closest thing to what you're building, and it's the one to study hardest.
- Leads every message with the "the test is now digital, your prep book is obsolete" angle — a genuinely strong, timely hook.
- Simulates the real exam interface (drag-and-drop, scrambled paragraph, cross-out, scratch pad, bookmark/review).
- Adaptive IRT engine + predicted scaled score (200–800) using the same methodology the DOE uses — this is their headline differentiator.
- Maps every wrong answer to a specific skill and tells the student what to drill next.
- Risk-reversal: 30-day "score improves or full refund" guarantee.
- Positions explicitly against tutors ($100–200/hr) and Kaplan ($449+) on price.

**ArgoPrep** — clean tiered subscription with a clear annual discount; low-friction 3-day trial; strong video library.

**TestPrepSHSAT.com** — uses **official** SHSAT content (a real trust signal), offers a genuinely free digital tier, and has a strong affordability/equity narrative ("scored under $100 and beat the kid who paid $4,000").

**TestingMom** — enormous question bank, household/multi-child membership, lifetime option, and bundled bonus programs that inflate perceived value. (Caution: widely reported dark-pattern billing/cancellation complaints — an easy thing for you to beat on trust.)

**Caddell Prep** — diagnostic-first flow, clear self-paced vs. tutoring ladder, and an explicit "start in 6th grade" pathway (directly relevant to your daughter's cohort).

**Kaplan / Princeton Review** — brand trust, score guarantees, structured curricula, and instructor credibility ("our teachers scored top marks / attended specialized high schools"). Their weakness is price and rigidity.

**Local NYC tutors (Kweller, Khan's, SamSchool)** — community trust, published admission results (e.g. "80% of our students admitted"), and human accountability. Hard to match online, but also their cost ceiling is your floor.

## 4. Pricing model patterns

- **Subscription (monthly/annual) dominates the digital tier.** It's the model you should default to. Annual plans are discounted ~30–50% vs monthly to lock in revenue.
- **Pay-per-test is rare and underused.** Almost nobody sells individual tests à la carte — they bundle. This is an open lane if you want a low-commitment on-ramp (e.g. $5–10 per full proctored digital test, or a 3-test pack).
- **Hybrid / one-time course fees** ($499–$4,750) are the tutoring world; high friction, high touch.
- **Freemium funnels** are standard: free diagnostic (HighScores), free question samples (TestingMom), free trial week (Kaplan, Khan's), free tier (TestPrepSHSAT). You will need one.
- **Score guarantees** are becoming table stakes at the premium end (HighScores, Princeton Review, some books). Powerful but only viable once you trust your content.

Rough digital-tier price anchors to position against:
- Budget: **$5–$50/mo** (TestPrepSHSAT, ArgoPrep)
- Premium digital: **$149–$299/mo** (HighScores.ai)
- The middle — **~$15–$60/mo for a polished, test-realistic, NYC-focused product** — is thinly occupied.

## 5. Where the gaps are — your disruption angles

You have two assets most competitors don't: **(a) real full-length SHSAT practice tests** (Forms A/B, 2023–2025) already in hand, and **(b) a "critical thinking" brand** that can differentiate from rote drilling.

**1. Beat HighScores.ai on price with comparable realism.**
They charge $149–$299/mo. If you can deliver a faithful *digital test interface* (drag-and-drop, scrambled paragraph, scratch pad, timer, review/flag) at $15–$40/mo, you undercut them 5–10× on the one feature that matters most in 2026. You don't need their full AI engine to win — you need test-day realism plus honest scoring.

**2. Sell what almost nobody sells: pay-per-test / test packs.**
A parent who just wants 4 realistic digital practice tests before October has no clean option between "free DOE PDF" and "$149/mo subscription." Offer single digital tests ($5–10) or a 3–6 test pack ($25–40), with subscription as the upsell. This is a near-empty lane.

**3. Lead with the digital-format message, like HighScores does — but cheaper.**
"The SHSAT is now digital and adaptive. Don't prep on paper." It's true, urgent, and most of the field (books, local tutors) can't answer it. Make your interface match the real test exactly.

**4. Honest, transparent scoring + skill diagnostics.**
A predicted scaled-score estimate and a per-skill breakdown ("you're losing the most points on proportional reasoning and revising/editing") is the highest-value feature you can ship after the test interface itself. You can approximate this without heavy ML at first.

**5. Trust as a wedge.**
TestingMom and others are dogged by billing/cancellation complaints. A transparent price, easy cancellation, and a free diagnostic builds the trust the incumbents have eroded. Your "school of critical thinking" framing supports a teach-the-reasoning (not teach-the-trick) brand.

**6. Start-early funnel for the 6th–7th grade cohort.**
Caddell and Kaplan both note families starting a year+ early. A low-cost "foundations" track for 6th graders (exactly your daughter's stage) is a long lifetime-value on-ramp that converts to full prep in 7th/8th grade.

## 7. Build implications (Django-friendly, beginner-appropriate)

The features that actually move the needle, in priority order — none require React to start:

1. **Realistic digital test player** — timer, section navigation, flag/review, cross-out, scratch pad, and the TEI question types (drag-and-drop, scrambled paragraph, fill-in-the-blank). This is the whole game. You can build this server-rendered in Django + lightweight JS first; reach for React only if/when interactions get heavy.
2. **Auto-scoring + a scaled-score estimate** (start with a simple raw→scaled lookup from your existing answer keys; refine later).
3. **Per-skill results breakdown** (tag every question by skill; report weak areas).
4. **Free diagnostic → paywall** for full tests and analytics (Stripe subscriptions + a simple per-test purchase option).
5. **Accounts + progress tracking** over time.

You already own the hardest-to-source asset — real, full-length SHSAT content. The competitive moat is wrapping it in a test-day-accurate digital experience and pricing it where the market has a hole.
