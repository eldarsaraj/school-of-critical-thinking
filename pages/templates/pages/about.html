About

I grew up in war-torn Bosnia and came out of it as a refugee with nothing. My schooling was bad; based on either the chaos of war or the incompetence of a post-communist social system. Public education was my only option (my parents didn't have money for private school, even if it existed), but I came out of it confused and frustrated. I didn't know what mattered, and how to do anything. Clarity came later, when I taught myself important things (like statistics, philosophy, logic, and math). My education didn't teach me how to think. I had to do it myself.

That path led me to a Ph.D. in philosophy, a decade of teaching critical thinking at the City University of New York, and published research on reasoning, education, and the ethics of upbringing. Philosophy trained me to take arguments apart down to first principles. It also made me allergic to bullshit, and most of what's sold as education is exactly that (I even became an expert in the topic of bovine-excrement-as-a-language, see here).

When my daughter reached the age for some of the standardized tests offered (first Hunter, then SHSAT), I realized something that I had known only in theory: from a parent's perspective, the test-prep industry is a bottomless pit of murky and extremely expensive options. Like mindless sheep, us parents easily succumb to peer pressure and a specific kind of NYC FOMO -- "if I don't sign up my child to an expensive prep, somebody else will and their child will be better". But few, if any, test prep companies really tell you the truth about what they're actually doing. How do they actually help your child? How do they structure their practice? What kind of learning feedback they employ? It's cool if their tutors are young Stuy graduates who themselves scored well on these tests, but, it's not enough. Standardized tests are backed by science: the prep for them should use the same methods.

Once I realized this, I did what felt natural to me (given my expertise and experience): I started collecting official tests and running statistical and linguistic analysis on their entire corpora so I could understand the underlying structure of these exams. I mapped reading complexity by year, classified every question by cognitive task, and tagged every wrong answer by the specific trap it sets. I did this for Hunter and SHSAT first, and more tests are coming.

What came out of that work is this platform. It took me half a year (including one whole humid NYC summer) to put it together, but I made it. I do two main things here: first, I publish research about standardized tests, providing parents and tutors with data-driven insights and actionable recommendations (I use a lot of graphs, charts, and an occasional photo collage with poor taste and lame attempts at humor). Second, I make diagnostic tests so you guys can check how prepared your child or student is for the test. Every diagnostic test mirrors the distribution of skills, difficulty, and traps of each real test. Every wrong answer is error-tagged. After each test, parents and tutors see exactly which reasoning mistakes made the most, and how to avoid them in the future.

Every Baseline Test on this platform is free for parents. I understand not all of you have the means to send your child to tutoring, and I'm doing my part to help build a more equitable world. Since I got mouths to feed too, I do charge for additional Benchmark tests (and targeted practice sets), but as you will see, they are affordable and provide real value.

Call me too optimistic, but I believe anything can be reverse-engineered to first principles and rebuilt from there. No matter how complex the skill, done this way, anybody can learn it. Most educational frameworks are built for a median student that doesn't exist. That's why they fail everyone else. What I'm building is the opposite: analytics down to the atomic level, so every child learns from exactly where they are.

If there's anything I can do to help, feel free to reach out.
