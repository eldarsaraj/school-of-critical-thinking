For Organizations

I started The School of Critical Thinking because I believe critical thinking is the most undervalued skill in most workplaces. Not communication. Not emotional intelligence. The ability to think clearly about hard problems under real pressure.

Most corporate training on this topic is shallow. It covers fallacy lists and communication frameworks. It doesn't teach people how to actually reason under uncertainty, how to decompose complex problems, or how to maintain judgment when working alongside AI systems that produce confident-sounding answers at zero cost.

I run talks and workshops that treat critical thinking as the serious professional competency it is. Built on the same intellectual foundation as the books and curriculum, but applied to real organizational problems and real stakes.

What I offer

Three formats, one foundation

Lunch and Learn

60 to 90 minutes

A single-topic session designed to seed interest, demonstrate what real critical thinking looks like, and give a team something concrete to take back to their work. High-impact, low-disruption. Works well as a standalone or as the opening to a longer engagement.

Workshop

Half-day or full day

Hands-on application of critical thinking frameworks to real organizational problems. Pre-mortems, decomposition exercises, working through paradoxes and decision traps with the team's own cases. Not a lecture. The goal is for people to leave with a different way of approaching their actual work.

Multi-Session Program

Structured cohort, bespoke length

A deeper engagement for leadership teams, decision-making groups, or teams integrating AI into their workflows. Structured around the organization's specific thinking challenges. Includes between-session work and ongoing practice.

What we cover

Signature themes

These are the topics that distinguish this work from generic L&D content. Each one is built around a real intellectual problem, not a framework with a catchy acronym.

  • Thinking with AI without letting it think for you

    Judgment, evaluation, and epistemic discipline in the age of large language models. What it means to maintain your own reasoning when working with systems that produce fluent, confident, sometimes wrong answers at zero cost.

  • The hidden architecture of bad decisions

    Paradoxes, traps, and the patterns behind organizational failure. Why reasonable people make systematically bad decisions, and what to do about it.

  • Pattern recognition for leaders

    Seeing what others miss in data, decisions, and team dynamics. The difference between a real signal and noise that looks like a signal.

  • Decomposition and clarity

    Breaking down complex problems before trying to solve them. Why most organizational confusion is a decomposition problem in disguise.

  • Reasoning under uncertainty

    Probabilistic thinking for people who don't think of themselves as statisticians. How to make good decisions when you can't know the outcome in advance.

The facilitator

Eldar Sarajlic

I am a philosopher and academic who has taught logic, critical thinking, and philosophy at the university level for over a decade. I founded The School of Critical Thinking to build the curriculum I couldn't find elsewhere: one that takes thinking seriously as a discipline, from age eight to the boardroom, on the same intellectual foundations.

My approach is rooted in the same tradition of truth-seeking as academic philosophy, but applied to the real problems that organizations face: decisions under uncertainty, reasoning about complex systems, and maintaining judgment when working alongside AI.
