Inside the Hunter Test

A Data-Driven Briefing to Help You Understand the Test

The Hunter High School test is a complex standardized test created by experts whose inner workings are not available to the wider public. Most parents rely on previous test-takers and tutors to guide them through preparation. Given the highly competitive nature of the admission process, this creates an imbalance between parents who have access to high-quality information about the test and those who don't. This is just a fancy way of saying that some parents can and others can't affort tutoring. Unless you have been living under a golden dome (or Jeff Bezos is your sugardaddy), you know how expensive test prep in NYC can be.

But what if we could use publicly available information to reverse-engineer the test and uncover its inner workings using data-driven analysis?

That's the mission of the School of Critical Thinking. Over the past several months, we have analyzed every aspect of the test — the skills it measures, the traps it sets, and the structure of its reading passages. Here's a taste of what we found.

From the briefing

41% of reading questions test inference — the ability to draw conclusions from what's implied, and not explicitly stated. That's more than word meaning, figurative language, and tone combined. If your child's prep isn't heavily focused on inference, you're missing the mark.

Not all passages are created equal. Our analysis of sentence length, vocabulary density, and syntactic complexity reveals which types of texts the Hunter test favors, and how wide the difficulty range really is. We'll show you what makes certain passages harder than others and what your child should be reading to prepare.

Using NLP (Natural Language Processing, for my fellow nerds), we measured how semantically similar wrong answers are to correct ones. The result is striking: wrong answers on the Hunter test aren't random: they are deliberately built around the meaning of the correct answer. This is how the test tricks students who "kind of" understand the passage but can't distinguish between close options. We'll break down exactly how these traps work and what to do about them.

This is just a sample. In the full briefing, we'll cover more of similar findings, section-by-section. We'll talk how this translates into prep strategy, how to evaluate test prep options, and what the data says about which skills can actually make a dent.

We're inviting parents to a free Zoom session to walk through our findings. Two sessions are available — pick the one that works for you.

BTW, this is not a tutoring pitch: we do not offer tutoring. Our job is analytics.

Who are we?

The School of Critical Thinking is a research and analytics organization that uses machine learning and NLP to reverse-engineer standardized tests — pulling apart question structures, score distributions, and difficulty patterns that aren't visible to the naked eye.

We don't sell test prep, and we don't have a tutoring program. What we offer is the analysis itself, presented clearly, so you can make your own decisions based on data, not on marketing from companies that profit from your anxiety.

The briefing is led by Eldar Sarajlic, Ph.D. — Associate Professor at the City University of New York, published researcher on reasoning and education, and the person behind the data analysis you'll see in the session. Also, this briefing is free.
